# GoodTunes® — Capabilities

GoodTunes® is a fan-first music player built around the idea that a song is a **structured object** — audio, lyrics, credits, and the gear behind it — not just an MP3. Fans buy albums directly from the artist (no streaming-service middleman), listen in an Apple-Music-quality player on web and native, and discover the musicians, instruments, and brands behind every track. Artists get richer credits, a real listening relationship with their fans, and a new affiliate revenue stream through the gear they actually use.

A living, investor-facing catalog of what GoodTunes has **actually shipped**. Read this when you need to speak to investors, partners, or labels about the product as it exists today — not what's on the roadmap. For future-phase plans and deep-dives, see [`roadmap.md`](./roadmap.md).

This catalog reads in two lenses:

- **[For fans](#for-fans)** — what fans can do in the player today.
- **[For the operator / investor](#for-the-operator--investor)** — the admin tooling and operational process that lets a tiny team run the whole business. This is the "look how much leverage one operator has" story: how a release goes from a single pasted link to a streaming, leak-proof, fully-credited, fully-priced product for sale — in minutes, without a developer.

# For fans

What fans actually see and do in the player today — buy direct from the artist, listen in an Apple-Music-quality experience, and discover the people and gear behind every track.

## Listening, search & library

Three lenses on one library, fast search, and the same Apple-Music chrome on phone, tablet, desktop, and native iOS/Android.

- **Native iOS + Android shell, store-ready.** GoodTunes ships as a Capacitor-wrapped iOS and Android app from the same web codebase — iOS bundle id `Io.GoGoods.music` (Android applicationId `com.gogoods_mobile`), dark splash + status bar in the brand palette, protected on-device song downloads (Android today; the iOS download button is gated off pending an App-Store rollout call, though the protection code is platform-agnostic) — audio is encrypted at rest with a per-device key held in the phone's hardware secret store (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore, secure-element backed where available), kept in private storage that's excluded from device backups and the iOS Files app, played back only from inside the app by decrypting in memory (never re-exported as a plain file), refused on a jailbroken/rooted device (where playback falls back to streaming), and automatically revoked when a fan no longer owns the album; the web app never downloads audio at all, and a single platform layer (`client/src/lib/platform.ts`) that hides the Chat tab on native and the **Buy** affordances on iOS so the app conforms to App Store guideline 3.1.1 (purchases happen on the web). The player's volume slider is hidden on iOS web (where volume is read-only) and on the native iPad app (where the hardware-volume bridge isn't reliable, so a dead control is hidden rather than shown); on the native iPhone app it stays visible and mirrors the hardware volume buttons. The full submission runbook — privacy nutrition labels, demo-account flow, deep-link verification, and reviewer notes — lives in [`app-store-submission.md`](./app-store-submission.md); the build mechanics live in [`native-builds.md`](./native-builds.md).
- **New-fan welcome sheet.** Free signups — fans who create an account before buying anything — see a one-time Apple-Music-style welcome sheet that explains what GoodTunes actually is: a private player for music you own, with early access before streaming and possible bonus/exclusive content. The second screen of the sheet offers a single ask: opt in to "notify me when new music drops." The sheet is shown once (a timestamp is stamped on dismiss), never shown to returning buyers or legacy-imported fans who go through the existing returning-fan welcome instead, and is fully iOS-native safe (zero Buy/price copy anywhere in it, so it passes App Store guideline 3.1.1). Fans can update the notification preference any time from Account settings.

- **Branded album-art placeholder.** Any album with no artwork (or a broken/deleted artwork URL) now shows a ghosted artist photo with the album name overlaid instead of the browser's broken-image "?" glyph — consistent across the fan card, full-screen player, mobile album hero, desktop Now Playing dock, mini-player capsule, provenance sheet thumbnail, and GoodDeed certificate. The server resolves the primary artist's photo alongside every album response so the placeholder is available on every surface without an extra round-trip.
- **View Albums / Songs / Artists.** Browse the library through three lenses on the same catalog, with album art and artist photos inline.
- **Your owned & preview releases show in your Library at any stage.** A release you actually own — a purchase, a comped/manually-granted copy, or an active preview — now appears in *your own* Collection even while it's still being prepped (staged) or held back as hidden, so you can find and play your copy before it goes public. The staged copy carries a subtle "Not yet released" marker on its tile and opens and plays through the same owner-only path as any other album you own. Nothing changes for anyone else: fans who don't own the release still see nothing — it stays out of the public catalog, search, share links, and checkout until it's actually released.
- **Branded cover placeholder — no more broken "?" tiles.** When an album has no cover art yet (or its stored image is missing), GoodTunes never shows the browser's broken-image glyph. Instead it renders one consistent on-brand treatment everywhere a cover appears — album cards, the mobile and desktop album pages, the player dock, the mini-player, the admin cover thumbnail, and the GoodDeed® certificate: the artist's profile photo, dimmed and desaturated behind a navy scrim with the album name laid over it, so it's obviously a placeholder and not the final art. If there's no artist photo either, it falls back to a brand-toned navy tile with the album name. The real cover takes over automatically the moment art is added, and the placeholder returns if the art is ever removed.
- **Pinch-to-zoom bonus photos.** Tapping a bonus photo on an album now opens a full-screen lightbox a fan can pinch- or double-tap-zoom and pan — and the moment they zoom in, GoodTunes swaps in the full-resolution original (every uploaded image keeps its untouched original alongside a fast ~1500px display version), so behind-the-scenes shots stay crisp up close without bogging down the feed. Oversized art is downsized automatically on upload (and existing art was backfilled), so GoodDeed share cards and printed certificates render reliably even from very large source images.
- **Secure adaptive bonus videos.** Album bonus videos now stream the same way masters do — encrypted, signed, adaptive-bitrate HLS that adjusts quality to the fan's connection, with the high-quality original kept privately as the source and never handed out as a downloadable file. Tap a video and it plays in-place; anything still encoding shows a brief "preparing" state instead of failing. Every video the operator adds — whether pasted as a URL or uploaded straight from a file — gets a real poster still auto-extracted on the server (a frame ~1 second in) so its tile shows a proper thumbnail from the moment it's saved instead of a black or blank first frame. Watch-through analytics (start, 25/50/75% milestones, complete, pause, seek) flow into the same event pipeline as audio so the team can see which bonus content fans actually watch. On the desktop album page the Music Videos section now reads like Apple Music: up to ten videos sit on a single horizontally-scrolled rail of larger cards (title as muted caption beneath each thumbnail), and when there are more than four a **See All** arrow on the heading flips the rail into a full grid and back. Hovering a video reveals a single play button in the card's bottom-right corner, and clicking (or pressing Enter/Space on) a card opens it in a full-screen player overlay that starts playing right away, matching the mobile tap-to-play; locked/unowned cards stay unplayable. The photos grid is unchanged.
- **Search and sort.** Fast search across albums, songs, and artists from a single field at the top of the library, with sort by artist or title.
- **Shuffle.** One-tap shuffle on any album, playlist, or auto-collection.
- **Favorite.** Heart a song or star an artist. Favorites are saved to the fan's GoodTunes account on the server, so they survive logout and follow the fan to a new phone or browser. Favorited artists' songs roll into the auto-built Favorites playlist (see below).
- **Playlists, including an auto-playlist from favorites.** Fans build their own playlists, and a virtual "Favorites" playlist is maintained automatically — favorited songs plus every song by a favorited artist, deduped, freshest first.
- **Play Next / Play Last.** Queue any song to play immediately after the current track, or drop it at the end of the queue.
- **AirPlay to Apple TV / HomePod.** On iPhone and iPad, when an AirPlay target is nearby the player shows Apple's real AirPlay control — on the iPhone full-screen player, on iPad/desktop in the full-screen Now Playing surface, and now right in the iPad/desktop bottom Player dock (to the right of the lyrics control, mirroring Apple Music's dock); one tap opens the native iOS device picker and routes the currently-playing audio to the chosen speaker or TV. The button only appears when a target is actually available — so it never looks broken — and stays hidden on Android, desktop browsers, and in-app browsers. Playback keeps using the same encrypted, signed stream, so masters still never leave as a file.
- **Background audio + lock-screen controls.** On the native apps, music keeps playing when the screen locks or the app is backgrounded, and the phone's own lock screen / Control Center now shows the album artwork, song title, and artist with working play/pause, next/previous, and a scrubber that tracks real playback position — drag the lock-screen scrubber and the in-app player follows, and vice-versa. On iPhone/iPad this runs through an in-tree native bridge that owns the iOS Now Playing info and forwards every transport button back into the player; on Android the same metadata surfaces as a media-style notification via the system WebView. Mobile web and the PWA get the same now-playing metadata and hardware-key transport where the browser supports it.
- **In-car playback — CarPlay + Android Auto.** On the native apps, plugging into (or wirelessly connecting to) a car shows GoodTunes on the vehicle's screen with the current album artwork, song, and artist, full play/pause · next/previous · scrubber transport, and a browsable "Up Next" list — tap any track in the car and it starts playing, with the in-app player and the car staying in lock-step both directions. It reuses the exact same now-playing metadata and player controls the lock screen already drives, so there's nothing extra to set up; audio still streams through the app's encrypted, signed pipeline (masters never leave as a file). CarPlay uses Apple's native Now Playing + list templates; Android Auto surfaces GoodTunes as a media app in the car's app picker.
- **Tablet + desktop responsive storefront.** Library, Search, Recents, Playlists, Account, and artist pages now reflow for tablet (≥768px, 3-column grids) and desktop (≥1024px, fixed left sidebar nav + 4–6 column grids, Apple-Music-web-style chrome) instead of stretching the 390px mobile layout. The floating bottom-nav pill stays on phones; desktop swaps in one identical left rail everywhere — storefront pages and individual album pages alike — that mirrors the mobile dock: a top group of Search · Home · Collection · Recents, then a "Library" header over Songs / Artists, then a "Playlists" header over the Playlists destination, using Apple Music's subtle rounded highlight on the active item (no more blue indicator bar, and the old Support / Notifications rows are gone). Songs and Artists deep-link straight into their dedicated detail views (`/collection/songs`, `/collection/artists`), your own playlists list below, and an Apple-Music-style account chip pinned at the bottom — the fan's photo (or initials) + name, with the unread-chat dot still riding the avatar. On storefront pages the chip opens a small popover menu (avatar + name header, Account settings, and a Sign Out that returns to the login page); on album pages the chip jumps straight to the account page. **/account is now a real desktop page** — it renders beside the rail (not the stretched 390px phone column) and reuses the same Account sections fans see on mobile (profile, orders, collections, linked logins, password, streaming service, sign-out). Native iOS/Android (Capacitor) always stays on the mobile shell, and the phone Account layout is unchanged. On desktop the now-playing surface is the same full-width bottom Player dock the album page uses — centered above the content area with shuffle/transport/repeat, an inline scrubber, and volume (and a 30-second-capped Preview scrubber when auditioning) — so the player reads identically whether you're browsing the storefront or sitting on an album page (it remains in place while the route changes).
- **Apple-Music-density desktop album page.** On laptop and desktop, every album opens into a full-width fan surface with a 280-pixel cover hero, artist + meta + description, and an Apple-Music-style track list — rose play triangle on hover, animated rose equalizer on the currently-playing row, hover-reveal "add to playlist" chip, a per-track **⋯ menu** that opens an Apple-Music context menu (Play Next, Play Last, Add to Playlist…, and Favorite / Unfavorite), and a tightened bottom Player dock sized to Apple's proportions. The dock's right cluster now mirrors Apple Music's anatomy — **Lyrics · AirPlay · Up Next · Volume · expand** — and the lyrics button slides a **right-side lyrics panel** in and out (Apple-Music style) — the album grid/page stays fully visible and reflows to make room rather than being covered by a full-screen takeover. The panel runs the same GoodSync™ karaoke surface as the phone player (active line sharp and centered, neighbors softly blurred/faded, auto-scroll, tap a line to seek), reading straight off the live player so opening or closing it never restarts the song. The lyrics button itself lights up in brand blue while the panel is open so its state is always clear. The new **Up Next** button opens the queue in that **same right-side rail** — lyrics and Up Next are mutually exclusive, so opening one closes the other — and lists the rest of the queue so a fan can jump straight to any upcoming track; it lights up in brand blue while open just like lyrics, and on narrower tablet widths it opens as the same full-screen sheet the lyrics view uses. The **AirPlay** button sits between them and only appears on iPad/desktop where an AirPlay target is actually available (hidden on Android, desktop browsers, and in-app browsers), opening the native iOS device picker. The rail now runs **full height** and sits **flush against the right and bottom window edges** like a native Apple-Music panel (a solid navy surface rounded only on its inner top corner, no more floating card), so the player dock and content area flow around it without a gap.
- **Apple-Music hover affordances on every album card.** On laptops and any pointer device, hovering an album card anywhere in the player — your Collection grid, the Recently-Played rail, an artist's GoodTunes releases and streaming shelves, and album results in search — dims the cover under a subtle scrim and reveals a circular **Play** button (bottom-left) plus a **"…" More** menu (bottom-right); clicking the card body still opens the album. The More menu mirrors the mobile album menu — **View GoodDeed®**, **View Provenance / Ownership**, **Add to Playlist** — and adds a new **Download GoodDeed PDF** straight from the card — it opens the certificate in an in-page viewer (music keeps playing, the page is never replaced) with a Download action — for any copy you own. Each affordance only shows when it applies (Play only on a playable GoodTunes release; GoodDeed/Provenance/Download only on owned copies). Touch phones are untouched — a tap just navigates to the album, with no overlay. All of these surfaces now render from one shared album-card component, so the look and behavior stay identical everywhere.
- **Encrypted adaptive streaming with per-session tokens.** Masters never leave our infrastructure as a downloadable file. Each play request mints a short-lived, user-bound token, and audio is delivered as encrypted adaptive-bitrate segments — Spotify-Web-grade leak resistance, while letting fans listen instantly on any connection.
- **Apple-style split nav + unified search across the player.** The fan-shell bottom nav now reads as a labeled three-tab pillow on the left (Home · Collection · Recents) plus a standalone search circle on the right — the same shape Apple Music's player wears, with the search control sized to match the tab pillow's height and a clean gap between them. **Home** is the owned-albums grid under a big "Home" title (no sort/filter scope bar), and **Collection** is now an Apple-Library-style tappable list — Songs, Artists, and Playlists rows over a **Recently Added** 2-up grid ordered by when each album was actually acquired. Songs and Artists open dedicated detail views with a sort popover in the top-right (Title / Artist for songs; A–Z / Z–A for artists); Playlists is no longer its own dock tab (it folds under Collection). The Chat tab is gone from the dock; its unread count folds into a small red dot on the account avatar in the Home/Collection top-right (one tap into /account). On phones the search circle now expands leftward in place into a search field right inside the dock (tap again to collapse) — focusing the input and raising the keyboard in the same tap, so the keyboard reliably appears on iOS — and surfaces Recently Searched on open. Search is a first-class destination: typing fans out a ranked mixed result list (Top Results, Artists, Albums, Songs, Bonus Content, Gear, Vendors, Labels, People, Playlists) with horizontal Apple-style category pills for drilling in, a Recently Searched chip rail on landing, and per-fan history that survives logout. A new server-backed **Recents** tab groups everything the fan opened — albums, songs, artists, gear — into Today / Yesterday / This Week / Earlier, with swipe-to-remove and Clear All, capped at 200 entries.
- **Profile always reads with a real name.** The Account page leads with the fan's full name and avatar initials. When a sign-in path leaves us no name to store — Apple "Hide My Email" can withhold it, and fans created before names were captured never had one — GoodTunes asks for it: new Google/Apple signups get a "Full name" field on the one-time finish-setup screen, and any existing fan with no name on file sees a gentle, dismissible "Add your name" prompt on their Account page. Saving updates the profile header and initials instantly; fans who already have a name are never prompted. Fans (and operators) can also set a profile photo from the Edit Profile screen — including **iPhone HEIC/HEIF photos**, which are now transcoded to JPEG in the browser before upload instead of being rejected, so a picture straight from an iPhone (Files, the share sheet, or AirDropped to a Mac) just works.
- **One-app shell across every fan surface.** Artist pages, album pages, the vendor sheet, and chat all wear the same Apple-Music-grade chrome — 44-pixel circular icon buttons (Apple-HIG minimum), a single shared bottom-nav clearance so content never slides under the floating nav and mini-player, and a steadier scroll-hide that no longer flickers on small momentum scrolls. The top and bottom bars now follow Apple's own header/footer treatment: at rest they're just a soft navy gradient fade so content scrolling behind stays legible with no hard edge, and they swap in a single frosted blur band only while you're actively doing something (search open, a menu or picker up), then melt back to the gradient — never two stacked blur layers fighting each other over a scrolling list. Every top-level sheet now closes with the same big Apple-style light-gray circular "X" in the top-right, and drill-down sheets (gear, vendor) offer a single unambiguous way out — one visible back chevron in the top-left that returns to the page you came from, with no competing "X". Ready for the upcoming Capacitor wrap so the App Store build looks intentional from screen one.

## The song as a structured object — credits, gear & lyrics

What makes GoodTunes more than a player: every track carries synced lyrics, per-performer credits, the exact gear used, and tappable links to buy it.

- **"Signed to {Label}" link on the artist page → fan label page.** Every fan-facing artist page now shows a small Apple-Music-style "Signed to {Label Name}" subhead above the artist's name when that artist is signed to a label, tappable straight through to a new fan label page at `/label/:id`. The label page shows the label's logo, name, and a grid of every GoodTunes release on that label (excluding hidden, prepping, and soft-deleted rows + streaming-only rows that aren't real GoodTunes releases). The existing `/label` partner dashboard route stays untouched.
- **Dynamic cross-linking across the catalog.** Every Vendor, Artist, Gear item, and Label is a first-class entity that links to every other. Tap a guitar inside a song's credits and you land on its vendor's page; tap the vendor and you see every artist on GoodTunes who plays their gear. The catalog acts like a graph, not a list of files.
- **Preview slider.** Every track carries a precision-trimmed 30-second preview window the artist sets visually against the waveform. Fans hear the artist's chosen hook anywhere a preview plays — search, share cards, free-tier teasers — instead of a hard-cut first 30 seconds.
- **GoodSync™ synced lyrics.** Lyrics scroll in time with the music in the Apple-Music-style overlay, with the active line in focus and surrounding lines softly blurred. Tapping any line seeks the song to that lyric — fans navigate the song by what's being sung, not by a timecode. The auto-sync workflow runs against any master length — when the master is too large for the forced-alignment provider's own size cap, GoodTunes transcodes a throwaway alignment-only copy on the fly so cues come back from any song, not just the short ones. **GoodSync now runs itself on upload:** the moment a freshly uploaded master finishes processing, GoodTunes automatically transcribes and time-aligns the lyrics, snaps the 30-second preview to the chorus, and flags instrumental or explicit tracks — all in the background, filling in only what the operator hasn't already set, so most releases arrive fan-ready with no manual step. The operator still gets a per-track "Re-run GoodSync" control to redo it on demand.
- **Chorus finder.** GoodTunes can locate a song's chorus and snap the 30-second preview window to it, so previews and share cards default to the part of the song that hooks people fastest. It works two ways: when the lyrics carry a labeled `[Chorus]` section, GoodTunes finds it instantly with no AI cost; when they don't, an AI pass reads the time-aligned GoodSync™ cues to find where the chorus begins. The operator gets it three places — as an opt-in follow-up right after aligning lyrics, as a one-pass option in the album-wide GoodSync wizard, and as an on-demand "Find the chorus" action on any single track's preview editor. It never guesses silently: when no chorus can be found with confidence the preview is left exactly as it was, and the operator is told honestly.
- **SuperCredits™.** Per-track credits that go far beyond Apple's writer-only list — every performer, the specific instrument they played on that take (down to "1973 Martin D-28"), tuning notes, and tappable affiliate links to buy the gear. Artists keep the lion's share of any sale. Credits turn into a real revenue stream, and a fan-discovery surface no other player offers.
- **Album-wide production credits.** Every album page (fan + admin) renders a grouped Produced / Mixed / Mastered / Engineered panel above the tracklist — each credited person is an avatar + inline-linked name that opens the same Person sheet song-level credits already use, free-text-only rows render as plain text, and the panel collapses past six role rows behind a Show-all toggle so long liner-notes don't bury the music.
- **Apple-style album footer.** Every album page (mobile + desktop) closes with an Apple-Music-grade credit footer: the full original release date ("February 16, 2010"), song count + total runtime ("13 songs, 58 minutes"), the record label, and the phonogram/copyright line ("℗ 2009 Brash Music"). The operator enters the exact original release date and the copyright credit in the album editor (Release + Metadata panels), and can pick the leading symbol per release — ℗ (sound recording, the default) or © (copyright) — so compilations and reissues read correctly. The Label field is a searchable, type-and-create picker (like the Genre field): operators search every existing label, keep "Independent" as the no-label default, or type a brand-new label name and create it inline without leaving the page — new labels appear everywhere across the app immediately. Picking or creating a label auto-fills an empty copyright line as "{year} {label}" (still fully editable, and it never overwrites a copyright line you've already written). When no exact date has been set the footer degrades gracefully to the bare release year, and the copyright line is hidden entirely when blank.
- **Every credited person is tappable, everywhere.** Wherever a person appears in credits — song-level SuperCredits™, the album-wide production panel, the desktop album view, and the admin credits panel — anyone with a real record is an Apple-Music-style tappable link to their Person sheet (About / Music / Gear), while people who are just free-text liner-notes stay plain text. Production-only roles that never touch a track (Mastered by, A&R, …) resolve too: their sheet opens on About with the credited role as the subtitle instead of dead-ending.
- **Person sheet shows the gear they actually played.** A credited musician's Person sheet (About / Music / Gear) now lists the instruments behind their credits: instruments GoodTunes has a record for are tappable rows that open the full gear page, while named-but-unlinked instrument credits (e.g. "Pedal Steel") still show as a plain row with their category, so a fan always sees what someone played even before an operator links the exact item. Vocals and production/engineering credits stay off the gear list. Scraped Apple Music boilerplate ("Listen to music by … on Apple Music.") is stripped from bios at import and never shows on the sheet.
- **Artist Rigs — tap a track's gear, see the whole setup.** When an artist documents the exact rig behind a take — the base instrument plus the strings, picks, capo and other pieces that complete it — fans see it right in a track's credits, with the same experience on phone and desktop. The gear shows as a tappable door inside "On this track" that opens a full rig page: the artist photo, the instrument as its own tappable gear card, every accessory (an accessory that GoodTunes carries is tappable too), and the other songs on the album made with the same rig. If GoodTunes carries the instrument the page shows **"Available from {vendor}"** to buy it directly; if not, a one-tap **"Request this rig"** lets the fan ask where to get it (no account needed). It's the gear-discovery payoff of SuperCredits™ made tangible — a fan can go from a song they love to the exact setup that made it in two taps.
- **Per-track credits, Apple song-page style.** Credits are now a per-track surface (the album-level "i"/info button next to Play is hidden). On mobile a fan opens a track's popover, on desktop the track's ⋯ menu, and chooses Credits to land on a surface that reads like Apple Music's song page: the song artwork on top, a Play control that plays just that one song, an **artist · album · date** line where tapping the album name returns to the album, and the three credit groups (Performing Artists, Composition & Lyrics, Production & Engineering) as circular avatars with a bold name over a grey role — all in GoodTunes navy/brand colors. Mobile is a single column; tablet/desktop flows the groups into balanced columns. Tapping any credited person still slides to their profile (About / Music / Gear) and back. Tracks with no credits never show the action, and clicking the track itself still plays it.
- **Apple-polished desktop credits card.** The desktop/iPad Album Credits and Song Credits cards read like Apple Music's: light, thin section rules, a close-X that fades away while you scroll the card and returns when you stop or reach the top, and an in-place person drill-down — tapping a credited person swaps the card to their profile (About / Music / Gear) with a back chevron top-left instead of tearing the card down, while the X still closes everything and the gear/vendor links keep working. People who are just a name and photo with no real profile stay plain text rather than dead-ending on an empty page.
- **Ernie Ball strings + PickWorld picks importable by URL.** Pasting an Ernie Ball string-set URL (with the product hash, e.g. `#P02217`) or a PickWorld product URL into the "Add from URL" gear importer now resolves the exact string set or pick — name, price, description, product photo, and SKU — without importing the page's generic "Compare" heading or brand-level metadata. Both brands are correctly wired as makers (not resellers), so the gear appears in the catalog under the right manufacturer slot.
- **D'Addario + Elixir strings importable by URL.** Pasting a D'Addario (`daddario.com`) or Elixir (`elixirstrings.com`) string-set product URL into the "Add from URL" gear importer now pulls the exact set — name, string gauge (e.g. `10-46`), catalog SKU, price, and product photo — and auto-files it under the **Strings** category instead of the brand-name junk those stores list as the product type. Both are correctly wired as makers (not resellers), so the strings land under the right manufacturer slot.
- **Gear photo galleries.** A piece of gear now shows more than a single thumbnail: vintage listings often carry 5–15 photos (front, back, headstock, case, wear), and the gear sheet renders them as a tidy swipeable thumbnail strip beside the hero shot. When an operator adds gear by pasting a listing URL, the importer pulls in the whole gallery (Shopify product photos, repeated social images, structured-data image sets), shows a pick-which-photos grid (everything selected by default), and rehosts each chosen shot to GoodTunes storage so it survives even if the original listing disappears.
- **Gear page that reads like an album.** Tapping a piece of gear — from a credit row or anywhere it's linked — now opens a full editorial surface instead of a cramped card: a full-bleed hero photo with the maker's logo+name chip, an artist note, an Overview write-up, a swipeable photo gallery with a pinch-to-zoom lightbox, a Specs card, a "Played by" rail of the artists on GoodTunes who use it, and "Where to buy" rows that split the brand globe from each vendor's direct product link. The same layout renders whether the gear opens as an in-album sheet or its own standalone page, so the experience is identical everywhere a fan finds it.
- **Artist Rigs.** A "Rig" is a named gear bundle — a base instrument plus the accessories that complete it (strings, picks, capo, tuning, settings, …) — that an artist can attach to a specific track with a per-track tweak note ("capo moved to the 2nd fret for this take"). Fans see the full rig under a track's credits: the base instrument links straight to its gear page, each accessory shows as a labeled chip, and the artist's tweak note sits underneath, so a fan can recreate the exact sound of a recording. Fernando Perdomo's folk-pop rig on *Waves* ("Beautiful Soul") is the first live example.
- **Gear bookmarks.** Fans can bookmark any piece of gear they spot inside SuperCredits™ — the guitar they noticed on track 3 lives in a dedicated Bookmarks page in their account, one tap away later.
- **Vendor chat (demo).** A Chat tab in the player lets a fan open a thread with the vendor behind any piece of gear directly from its credit row — the instrument is auto-attached as a preview card so the conversation starts in context. Today this is a working demo of the UX (threads live on-device, replies are canned); real vendor accounts and live routing are scoped for the next phase.
- **Stream-elsewhere credited tracks.** _(Fan-facing handoff is currently turned off across the app for the iOS App Store build — the credits below still show, but the "Stream this" / per-track handoff and the service picker are hidden everywhere, reversible from one flag. The admin link-resolution and stored deep links described here are unaffected.)_ GoodTunes can carry the full SuperCredits™ for a track it doesn't host — every writer, performer, instrument, and gear link — and hand the fan off to their own streaming service to actually listen. ## Buying, owning & gifting

Buy direct from the artist, get a numbered GoodDeed collectible, share or gift it, and track physical fulfillment — all leak-proof.

- **Post-purchase gift hub.** Right after checkout the Welcome screen presents three explicit choices — **Gift this**, **Keep it**, and **Decide later** — instead of a buried toggle. Choosing "Gift this" opens a recipient form (name, email, optional personal note) and creates a claim link the fan can share; the album + GoodDeed transfer to the recipient's account when they tap it. "Decide later" persists a 7-day pending-gift flag on the order and arms a reminder scheduler that emails the buyer before the window closes. Buyers can cancel an un-claimed gift from their Orders page at any time (disabled once the recipient has claimed or the physical record is in fulfillment). For vinyl orders, the Buy sheet also offers a **Ship to someone else** toggle that routes the record directly to a recipient's address at checkout time — downloads and the GoodDeed stay with the buyer so they can gift or keep those independently.

- **Gift one copy from a multi-copy order, keep the rest.** When a fan buys two or more copies of the same release, they no longer have to gift the whole order or nothing. Both the post-purchase Welcome screen and the Orders page now break the order into per-copy cards — each copy shows its own live state (yours to keep / gift sent and pending / claimed / cancelled) and its own self-serve controls: gift a single copy to a named recipient, copy or resend the claim link, change the recipient within 24 hours (pre-claim), or cancel an un-claimed gift (disabled once the record is in fulfillment). Whole-order gifting and per-copy gifting are mutually exclusive, so a fan can't double-gift the same copy; a cancelled copy simply returns to the buyer's collection.

- **Personalize each donation gift box after checkout.** When a fan buys one or more "fan-chooses-amount" custom add-on boxes (such as Nightbirde's **Gift of Hope**), GoodTunes no longer asks *who each box is for* at checkout — the Buy sheet just collects how many boxes and how much to give, with two reassurance lines that the recipients are chosen afterward. Right after paying, the Welcome screen guides the buyer through a **"Who's the gift for?"** step, once per box ("Box 1 of 3"): for each box they pick either **"[Foundation] chooses"** (the charity decides who receives it — only an optional message) or **"Someone I know"** (the recipient's name, phone, shipping address, plus an optional giver name and message). A buyer who closes the step can reopen it any time from their **Orders** page, which shows live status like "2 of 3 boxes personalized." No copy anywhere implies the fan has to email an address to anyone — the next step is always in the app. Whoever fulfills the boxes (the foundation) sees each box's assignment — "chooses" or the named recipient with the full shipping address and message — right on the order view they already use, so a box can actually be shipped to a real person; that recipient information is visible only to the assigned fulfiller and to admins. The whole flow is generic — any charity's custom add-on reuses the same machinery under its own name.

- **Dedicated launch storefront at `store.goodtunes.music`.** A fan who lands on the store host drops straight into the featured release's preview-and-purchase page — no menus, no login wall to look around — and the entire sign-in, preview, and embedded-checkout flow works there exactly as it does on the main fan host, so a release can be promoted on its own clean URL (used for the Nightbirde "Hope" launch). Deep links to any individual album keep working as before.

- **Two-part artist/album share links at `get.goodtunes.music/<artist>/<album>`.** Every release can carry a two-part human-readable URL — `get.goodtunes.music/nightbirde/its-ok` — that opens straight onto the same preview-and-buy page fans get from a normal album link, with no login wall. The artist slug lives on the artist's person record (shared across all that artist's releases) and the album slug lives on the release — both set from a two-field panel on the album editor, each independently saveable on blur with live normalization, uniqueness checks, reserved-word guards, and one-tap "Suggest" buttons. The artist slug can also be set or cleared directly from an "Artist URL" panel on the People admin page, so operators can claim an artist's URL before any albums exist or while managing an artist with many releases. The full link is shown in the panel as soon as both halves are saved, with Copy and Open buttons. The link rich-unfurls in iMessage/Slack/email with the album's real cover, title, and artist. Slugs only resolve for releases that are actually for sale — hidden, still-prepping, or removed releases return a normal not-found, so a leaked slug can never expose an unreleased album.

- **Legacy gogoods.com GoodDeed certificates regenerated.** Every imported gogoods.com owner gets a fresh, GoodTunes-branded GoodDeed certificate PDF — keyed off the original certificate number so #042 stays #042 — opened in an in-page viewer (with a Download action) from the "Download GoodDeed" action on each imported copy, so the music never stops and signed-in owners never hit a "Sign in required" page. Paper size auto-picks Letter or A4 from the fan's shipping country, and the admin print queue tags these as "Legacy" so operators don't accidentally re-print certs fans already physically own.

- **Dedicated signed-certificate step in the Buy sheet.** When an album offers a signed-cert add-on, the per-copy certificate choices now appear on their own named step inside the Buy sheet — not buried in the product scroll. After choosing a format, quantity, and booklet, the fan taps "Continue to checkout" and the sheet advances to a clean cert screen: album art header, the offer description, a live "Only N left" scarcity nudge, a per-copy signed/unsigned toggle for every copy being purchased (scarcity-capped so a fan can never oversell the run), an optional certificate-name field for digital GoodDeed copies, a "Checkout — $Total" button, and a prominent "Skip — no certificate" escape. Albums with no active cert add-on skip the step entirely. Pricing, Stripe checkout, and the rest of the flow are unchanged.

- **Confirm the name on your digital GoodDeed certificate.** When a fan opens the in-page viewer for a digital-only GoodDeed (a copy without the physical signed-cert add-on), the certificate now shows the name it will print — pre-filled from the fan's own account name — with an **Edit** control to correct it. This matters most for imported gogoods.com owners, whose certificate would otherwise carry an old username; they can now type the real name they want, save it, and the viewer re-renders the PDF on the spot. The chosen name sticks for every later download. Copies that include the physical signed certificate keep their existing operator-driven confirmation flow untouched.

- **Old gogoods.com cert QR codes still work.** Fans holding a physical signed GoodDeed certificate from the earlier gogoods.com era can scan its printed QR code and land on their record's live GoodTunes provenance page — instead of the dead gogoods.com link it points at today. GoodTunes maps each old per-copy code back to the copy the fan now owns and forwards the scan straight to its provenance page (no sign-in wall, no app stop). If a code can't be matched — for example an extra copy that never had its own certificate — the fan gets a friendly "let's find your GoodDeed" lookup page that points them to their library, never a 404.

- **Store-wide, leak-proof previews.** Fans (and visitors) can preview any song on any GoodTunes release before buying — even on hidden or unreleased albums, provided they have the link. Audio is delivered as the same encrypted adaptive-bitrate segments as the full master, but is hard-capped at the artist's chosen 30-second window on the server — so a preview session never exposes the full track file. Non-owners are never offered a "Stream on Spotify/Apple" handoff for a hosted GoodTunes track, keeping the focus on buying the high-res master. An operator can **hide an individual track's preview** (the Master tile's "Hide preview" toggle, with an optional auto-unhide date) — those tracks still show their number and title in the list, Apple-style, but render as a grayed, non-tappable row with no runtime and no play affordance, and **Play and Shuffle skip straight past them** to the next available track. This treatment holds for *everyone* on the album page — fans, visitors, and owners (including the operator) alike — so a track held back as not-yet-released stays a dead row until its auto-unhide date arrives. (The server also independently refuses to sign hidden previews for non-owners, so one can't be auditioned even with a direct link.)

This same rich page — never a bare "Coming Soon" card — is what every public link to a still-**prepping** release now resolves to: the one-word fan slug (`get.goodtunes.music/<release>`), the two-part artist/album slug, and the `/staging` family link all land on it in this notify-only mode. - **Every release lands like an unlock on any share host — including the player host.** Any release a logged-out visitor doesn't own opens as the real album page — hero art, tracklist, the centered player dock, and locked bonus videos — fronted by an auto-opening, centered offer card, so buying *feels* like unlocking the page. - **Forgiving sign-in on every surface.** Both password fields on the login screen (Sign In and Create Account) have an eye/eye-off reveal toggle so a pasted or autofilled value can be read before submitting — removing the most common source of the "Invalid password" false failure. - **GoodTunes Shopify+ — sell on your own Shopify, we run manufacturing.** A third distribution mode alongside Direct (GoodTunes does everything, fan-funded) and Shopify (they sell, we add the digital unlock). With **GoodTunes Shopify+** the customer sells the record on *their own* Shopify store and gets the full GoodTunes production pipeline behind it — upload art + audio, preflight, print-ready PDFs, press routing, GoodDeed — while every GoodTunes fan surface is switched off for that release (no preview/purchase page, no fan checkout, and no fan-sale pool or early-cut evaluation). **Signed GoodDeed** and **fulfillment** are each a simple album-level on/off: with fulfillment on, the operator assigns a warehouse from a dropdown and the album editor shows the exact **per-unit fulfillment price** (partner rate + markup) for the customer to load into their Shopify pricing; with it off, GoodTunes dropships the finished run to the customer. - **Prepaid manufacturing ledger (ACH, staged).** A GoodTunes Shopify+ album carries a per-album **manufacturer payment ledger** that mirrors a real pressing quote: the operator uploads the manufacturer's quote PDF(s) and hand-keys an open-ended series of steps (test-pressing/setup deposit, vinyl run, overage & freight, fulfillment, GoodDeed legs), each with an amount owed. Each step is paid by **ACH bank-debit that routes straight to the manufacturer** (~$5 fee cap) over the same transfer rail GoodTunes already uses for press invoices — no card, no new payout plumbing — with an optional GoodTunes margin/handling line per step (default off, so it's a clean pass-through). The ledger totals **quoted / paid / outstanding** across the whole series. **Anyone with album-level access** — label, manager, or artist who holds the `manage_payouts` verb on that release — can make the payments, not just the label.

Because the customer's own Shopify sends its own buyer notifications, GoodTunes stays silent to the fan (no GoodTunes-branded shipping email) and simply gets the record made and shipped.

- **Shopify+ "Submitted to press" release status.** Shopify+ releases get a three-step release ladder — Prepping → **Submitted to press** → Released — so GoodTunes can formally submit the vinyl package to the press for review (and keep refining it) before the digital release flips on via the artist's presale. The press portal shows a "Submitted for review" badge on submitted albums, distinct from merely having a pressing order assigned; fans see nothing until Released, and status moves forward or back at any time.

- **Shopify+ can still hand fans the app — as a per-product opt-in.** Even on a GoodTunes Shopify+ release (where GoodTunes isn't the seller), the operator can flip a per-product **"Also mint the GoodTunes digital unlock + GoodDeed"** toggle so buyers of that Shopify product still get the in-app album unlock, a redemption code, and a GoodDeed number — exactly like plain Shopify mode. These land as **external** orders that stay out of GoodTunes revenue and never trigger an artist/label payout (the customer collected the money on their own Shopify) and still never accrue the fan-sale pool. Left off (the default for a Shopify+ product), the order takes the fulfillment-only path above. Signed GoodDeed and fulfillment stay the same album-level on/off switches, so a release can offer the unlock without shipping through GoodTunes — or ship through GoodTunes without offering the unlock.

- **Gifting.** Fans can buy an album as a gift for someone else. The recipient gets a unique link to claim their GoodDeed and add the album to their library.

- **Delete your account from inside the app.** Every fan can permanently delete their GoodTunes account from **Account → Privacy → Delete My Account** (behind a plain-language confirm). Deleting removes their profile and personal data — favorites, playlists, library, and sign-in (password and any Google/Apple links) — and signs them out everywhere; past orders are retained for legal and accounting records. This satisfies Apple's and Google's requirement that an app offering account creation also lets users delete their account in-app.

- **Any fan can self-serve a working sign-in link.** A fan who can't get into their account — forgot their password, never set one, signed in with Google/Apple originally, or carried over from the pre-June-2026 catalog — can tap **"Can't sign in?"** on the login screen, enter their email, and get a one-tap sign-in link emailed to them with no password required. The same link is sent from the **Forgot password?** path for any fan without a password, so neither entry point dead-ends. (To avoid leaking who has an account, the on-screen response is always the same neutral confirmation whether or not the email matches a fan.) New fans signing up with an emailed 6-digit code never lose their place either: the code-entry screen survives a refresh or a trip to the inbox, and an **"Already have a code?"** link on the sign-in screen always gives an emailed code somewhere to be entered.

- **Signing up with an email that already has an account no longer dead-ends.** If a fan tries to create a new account with an email that's already registered — common for the passwordless fans carried over from the old catalog — the sign-up screen now recognizes it immediately, *before* sending any code, and shows a short "you already have an account" screen with one tap to **email yourself a sign-in link** and another to **sign in with a password**. The same recovery is offered when someone tries Google/Apple sign-in with an email that already exists: instead of a fleeting message telling them to "sign in first," they get a clear, lasting prompt with the one-tap link right there. Previously these fans hit a confusing loop that burned verification codes and ended on a misleading "that code didn't match" error.

- **Returning legacy fans land back on their real collection — even after a forced Apple/Google re-sign-in.** Thousands of fans carried over from the old catalog have an email on file but never set a password and never linked Google or Apple. Before, when an app update forced them to sign in again with Google or Apple, they'd land in a brand-new empty account instead of the library they actually own. Now, when a fan signs in with a social login whose verified email matches one of these credential-less imported accounts, GoodTunes recognizes it and reconnects them to their real collection automatically. Apple "Hide My Email" fans — who arrive with a masked relay address that can't be matched on sight — get a short, friendly **"Connect your existing account"** step: enter the real email you signed up with, confirm a 6-digit code, and your library, orders, and certificates reattach (or tap **"I'm new here"** to start fresh). Fans who *do* have a real password or an already-linked social login are never auto-merged this way — they're still asked to sign in with what they have, so an account with a real credential can never be taken over. Fans who were already stranded in a duplicate empty account before this fix are reconciled automatically behind the scenes, with their collection kept on the original library account.

## Catalog Management

The server resolves the share link server-side (SSRF-safe: private-IP and http:// links are blocked before any fetch) and streams the zip directly into the same extraction pipeline.

- **Asset management.** High-res masters, artwork, and bonus content are stored and served securely.
