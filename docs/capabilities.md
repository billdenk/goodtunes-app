# GoodTunes® — Capabilities

GoodTunes® is a fan-first music player built around the idea that a song is a **structured object** — audio, lyrics, credits, and the gear behind it — not just an MP3. Fans buy albums directly from the artist (no streaming-service middleman), listen in an Apple-Music-quality player on web and native, and discover the musicians, instruments, and brands behind every track. Artists get richer credits, a real listening relationship with their fans, and a new affiliate revenue stream through the gear they actually use.

A living, investor-facing catalog of what GoodTunes has **actually shipped**. Read this when you need to speak to investors, partners, or labels about the product as it exists today — not what's on the roadmap. For future-phase plans and deep-dives, see [`roadmap.md`](./roadmap.md).

This catalog reads in two lenses:

- **[For fans](#for-fans)** — what fans can do in the player today.
- **[For the operator / investor](#for-the-operator--investor)** — the admin tooling and operational process that lets a tiny team run the whole business. This is the "look how much leverage one operator has" story: how a release goes from a single pasted link to a streaming, leak-proof, fully-credited, fully-priced product for sale — in minutes, without a developer.

# For fans

What fans actually see and do in the player today — buy direct from the artist, listen in an Apple-Music-quality experience, and discover the people and gear behind every track.

## Listening, search & library

Three lenses on one library, fast search, and the same Apple-Music chrome on phone, tablet, desktop, and native iOS/Android.

- **Native iOS + Android shell, store-ready.** GoodTunes ships as a Capacitor-wrapped iOS and Android app from the same web codebase — bundle id `fm.goodtunes.player`, dark splash + status bar in the brand palette, on-device song downloads so albums play offline, and a single platform layer (`client/src/lib/platform.ts`) that hides the Chat tab on native and the **Buy** affordances on iOS so the app conforms to App Store guideline 3.1.1 (purchases happen on the web). The full submission runbook — privacy nutrition labels, demo-account flow, deep-link verification, and reviewer notes — lives in [`app-store-submission.md`](./app-store-submission.md); the build mechanics live in [`native-builds.md`](./native-builds.md).
- **View Albums / Songs / Artists.** Browse the library through three lenses on the same catalog, with album art and artist photos inline.
- **Pinch-to-zoom bonus photos.** Tapping a bonus photo on an album now opens a full-screen lightbox a fan can pinch- or double-tap-zoom and pan — and the moment they zoom in, GoodTunes swaps in the full-resolution original (every uploaded image keeps its untouched original alongside a fast ~1500px display version), so behind-the-scenes shots stay crisp up close without bogging down the feed. Oversized art is downsized automatically on upload (and existing art was backfilled), so GoodDeed share cards and printed certificates render reliably even from very large source images.
- **Search and sort.** Fast search across albums, songs, and artists from a single field at the top of the library, with sort by artist or title.
- **Shuffle.** One-tap shuffle on any album, playlist, or auto-collection.
- **Favorite.** Heart a song or star an artist. Favorites are saved to the fan's GoodTunes account on the server, so they survive logout and follow the fan to a new phone or browser. Favorited artists' songs roll into the auto-built Favorites playlist (see below).
- **Playlists, including an auto-playlist from favorites.** Fans build their own playlists, and a virtual "Favorites" playlist is maintained automatically — favorited songs plus every song by a favorited artist, deduped, freshest first.
- **Play Next / Play Last.** Queue any song to play immediately after the current track, or drop it at the end of the queue.
- **AirPlay to Apple TV / HomePod.** On iPhone, when an AirPlay target is nearby the player shows Apple's real AirPlay control; one tap opens the native iOS device picker and routes the currently-playing audio to the chosen speaker or TV. The button only appears when a target is actually available — so it never looks broken — and stays hidden on Android, desktop, and in-app browsers. Playback keeps using the same encrypted, signed stream, so masters still never leave as a file.
- **Tablet + desktop responsive storefront.** Library, Search, Recents, Playlists, Account, and artist pages now reflow for tablet (≥768px, 3-column grids) and desktop (≥1024px, fixed left sidebar nav + 4–6 column grids, Apple-Music-web-style chrome) instead of stretching the 390px mobile layout. The unified bottom player console stays on phones; desktop swaps in one identical left rail everywhere — storefront pages and individual album pages alike — that reads Search · Collection (with Albums / Songs / Artists indented under it) · Playlists · Recents, using Apple Music's subtle rounded highlight on the active item (no more blue indicator bar, and the old Support / Notifications rows are gone). The Collection sub-items deep-link straight into the matching tab (Songs and Artists open `/collection` already switched to that lens), your own playlists list below, and an Apple-Music-style account chip pinned at the bottom — the fan's photo (or initials) + name, with the unread-chat dot still riding the avatar. On storefront pages the chip opens a small popover menu (avatar + name header, Account settings, and a Sign Out that returns to the login page); on album pages the chip jumps straight to the account page. **/account is now a real desktop page** — it renders beside the rail (not the stretched 390px phone column) and reuses the same Account sections fans see on mobile (profile, orders, collections, linked logins, password, streaming service, sign-out). Native iOS/Android (Capacitor) always stays on the mobile shell, and the phone Account layout is unchanged. The Mini Player anchors bottom-right of the content area at desktop widths.
- **Apple-Music-density desktop album page.** On laptop and desktop, every album opens into a full-width fan surface with a 280-pixel cover hero, artist + meta + description, and an Apple-Music-style track list — rose play triangle on hover, animated rose equalizer on the currently-playing row, hover-reveal "add to playlist" chip, and a tightened bottom Player dock sized to Apple's proportions. The dock's lyrics button slides a **right-side lyrics panel** in and out (Apple-Music style) — the album grid/page stays fully visible and reflows to make room rather than being covered by a full-screen takeover. The panel runs the same GoodSync™ karaoke surface as the phone player (active line sharp and centered, neighbors softly blurred/faded, auto-scroll, tap a line to seek), reading straight off the live player so opening or closing it never restarts the song. The lyrics button itself lights up in brand blue while the panel is open so its state is always clear. On narrower medium/tablet-width screens (portrait iPads, split laptop windows) where there isn't room for the side panel, the same button opens the identical karaoke surface as a full-screen lyrics view over the album instead — so the lyrics button is never a dead control at any width. The hero CTAs adapt to ownership: owners see **Play + Shuffle**; non-owners see a **rose-outline Play pill** that auditions 30-second previews of each track and auto-advances through the album (with a "Preview" badge and a 30-second-capped scrubber in the bottom dock), next to a **rose-filled Buy pill** that shows the bundle price at rest, swaps to "Buy Now" on hover, and launches checkout — the same per-row hover triangle also fires a 30-second preview, and a copy-link button next to the CTAs gives fans and artists a shareable album URL they can paste into socials, email, or DMs. Hovering the Buy pill (or tapping on a touch surface) reveals an **Add signed GoodDeed +$X** chip the fan can toggle in before checkout, which hands the selection through to the Buy sheet with the add-on pre-checked. When a preview session walks the entire album to its end, the Buy sheet auto-opens so the moment closes with a clear CTA instead of dead silence. The mobile-web album page now matches this preview-first behavior — on Safari / mobile browsers, the Play button auditions the album rather than forcing a login or app hop.
- **Apple-Music hover affordances on every album card.** On laptops and any pointer device, hovering an album card anywhere in the player — your Collection grid, the Recently-Played rail, an artist's GoodTunes releases and streaming shelves, and album results in search — dims the cover under a subtle scrim and reveals a circular **Play** button (bottom-left) plus a **"…" More** menu (bottom-right); clicking the card body still opens the album. The More menu mirrors the mobile album menu — **View GoodDeed®**, **View Provenance / Ownership**, **Add to Playlist** — and adds a new **Download GoodDeed PDF** straight from the card for any copy you own. Each affordance only shows when it applies (Play only on a playable GoodTunes release; GoodDeed/Provenance/Download only on owned copies). Touch phones are untouched — a tap just navigates to the album, with no overlay. All of these surfaces now render from one shared album-card component, so the look and behavior stay identical everywhere.
- **Encrypted adaptive streaming with per-session tokens.** Masters never leave our infrastructure as a downloadable file. Each play request mints a short-lived, user-bound token, and audio is delivered as encrypted adaptive-bitrate segments — Spotify-Web-grade leak resistance, while letting fans listen instantly on any connection.
- **Unified bottom player console (mobile) + unified search across the player.** On phones and tablets the fan-shell bottom chrome is now a single rounded glass console pinned at the bottom: a now-playing row (album art, title, artist, with heart / play-pause / skip-next), a hairline blue progress bar beneath it, and a four-item nav row — Collection · Playlists · Recents · Search. It replaces the old separate mini-player capsule and split nav (three-tab pillow + standalone search circle), so there's one surface instead of three stacked pieces; tapping the now-playing row (anywhere but the transport buttons) opens the full Player, and when nothing is playing the console collapses to just the nav row. Search is the fourth nav item — tapping it raises the same inline search overlay right in place (focusing the input and raising the keyboard in the same tap, so the keyboard reliably appears on iOS; we never navigate to a /search route on phones) and surfaces Recently Searched on open. The Chat tab is gone from the dock; its unread count folds into a small red dot on the account avatar in the Collection top-right (one tap into /account). Desktop (lg+ web) is unchanged — it keeps the left sidebar nav with the bottom-right Mini Player. Search is a first-class destination: typing fans out a ranked mixed result list (Top Results, Artists, Albums, Songs, Bonus Content, Gear, Vendors, Labels, People, Playlists) with horizontal Apple-style category pills for drilling in, a Recently Searched chip rail on landing, and per-fan history that survives logout. A new server-backed **Recents** tab groups everything the fan opened — albums, songs, artists, gear — into Today / Yesterday / This Week / Earlier, with swipe-to-remove and Clear All, capped at 200 entries.
- **One-app shell across every fan surface.** Artist pages, album pages, the vendor sheet, and chat all wear the same Apple-Music-grade chrome — 44-pixel circular icon buttons (Apple-HIG minimum), a single shared bottom-nav clearance so content never slides under the floating nav and mini-player, and a steadier scroll-hide that no longer flickers on small momentum scrolls. The top and bottom bars now follow Apple's own header/footer treatment: at rest they're just a soft navy gradient fade so content scrolling behind stays legible with no hard edge, and they swap in a single frosted blur band only while you're actively doing something (search open, a menu or picker up), then melt back to the gradient — never two stacked blur layers fighting each other over a scrolling list. Every top-level sheet now closes with the same big Apple-style light-gray circular "X" in the top-right, and drill-down sheets (gear, vendor) offer a single unambiguous way out — one visible back chevron in the top-left that returns to the page you came from, with no competing "X". Ready for the upcoming Capacitor wrap so the App Store build looks intentional from screen one.

## The song as a structured object — credits, gear & lyrics

What makes GoodTunes more than a player: every track carries synced lyrics, per-performer credits, the exact gear used, and tappable links to buy it.

- **"Signed to {Label}" link on the artist page → fan label page.** Every fan-facing artist page now shows a small Apple-Music-style "Signed to {Label Name}" subhead above the artist's name when that artist is signed to a label, tappable straight through to a new fan label page at `/label/:id`. The label page shows the label's logo, name, and a grid of every GoodTunes release on that label (excluding hidden, prepping, and soft-deleted rows + streaming-only rows that aren't real GoodTunes releases). The existing `/label` partner dashboard route stays untouched.
- **Dynamic cross-linking across the catalog.** Every Vendor, Artist, Gear item, and Label is a first-class entity that links to every other. Tap a guitar inside a song's credits and you land on its vendor's page; tap the vendor and you see every artist on GoodTunes who plays their gear. The catalog acts like a graph, not a list of files.
- **Preview slider.** Every track carries a precision-trimmed 30-second preview window the artist sets visually against the waveform. Fans hear the artist's chosen hook anywhere a preview plays — search, share cards, free-tier teasers — instead of a hard-cut first 30 seconds.
- **GoodSync™ synced lyrics.** Lyrics scroll in time with the music in the Apple-Music-style overlay, with the active line in focus and surrounding lines softly blurred. Tapping any line seeks the song to that lyric — fans navigate the song by what's being sung, not by a timecode. The auto-sync workflow runs against any master length — when the master is too large for the forced-alignment provider's own size cap, GoodTunes transcodes a throwaway alignment-only copy on the fly so cues come back from any song, not just the short ones.
- **Chorus finder.** GoodTunes can identify a song's chorus from its lyric structure and audio and offer to snap the 30-second preview window to it, so previews and share cards default to the part of the song that hooks people fastest.
- **SuperCredits™.** Per-track credits that go far beyond Apple's writer-only list — every performer, the specific instrument they played on that take (down to "1973 Martin D-28"), tuning notes, and tappable affiliate links to buy the gear. Artists keep the lion's share of any sale. Credits turn into a real revenue stream, and a fan-discovery surface no other player offers.
- **Album-wide production credits.** Every album page (fan + admin) renders a grouped Produced / Mixed / Mastered / Engineered panel above the tracklist — each credited person is an avatar + inline-linked name that opens the same Person sheet song-level credits already use, free-text-only rows render as plain text, and the panel collapses past six role rows behind a Show-all toggle so long liner-notes don't bury the music.
- **Apple-style album footer.** Every album page (mobile + desktop) closes with an Apple-Music-grade credit footer: the full original release date ("February 16, 2010"), song count + total runtime ("13 songs, 58 minutes"), the record label, and the ℗ phonogram/copyright line ("℗ 2009 Brash Music"). The operator enters the exact original release date and the copyright credit in the album editor (Release + Metadata panels); when no exact date has been set the footer degrades gracefully to the bare release year, and the ℗ line is hidden entirely when blank.
- **Every credited person is tappable, everywhere.** Wherever a person appears in credits — song-level SuperCredits™, the album-wide production panel, the desktop album view, and the admin credits panel — anyone with a real record is an Apple-Music-style tappable link to their Person sheet (About / Music / Gear), while people who are just free-text liner-notes stay plain text. Production-only roles that never touch a track (Mastered by, A&R, …) resolve too: their sheet opens on About with the credited role as the subtitle instead of dead-ending.
- **Gear bookmarks.** Fans can bookmark any piece of gear they spot inside SuperCredits™ — the guitar they noticed on track 3 lives in a dedicated Bookmarks page in their account, one tap away later.
- **Vendor chat (demo).** A Chat tab in the player lets a fan open a thread with the vendor behind any piece of gear directly from its credit row — the instrument is auto-attached as a preview card so the conversation starts in context. Today this is a working demo of the UX (threads live on-device, replies are canned); real vendor accounts and live routing are scoped for the next phase.
- **Stream-elsewhere credited tracks.** GoodTunes can carry the full SuperCredits™ for a track it doesn't host — every writer, performer, instrument, and gear link — and hand the fan off to their own streaming service to actually listen. On an album that's earned the SuperCredits™ badge, each non-hosted track is tappable straight to the fan's service and the big control reads "Stream this"; an album with no credits gets a single "open the whole album on Spotify" handoff and no badge. The first handoff asks which service the fan prefers — Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, or Pandora — then remembers it — saved to their account so it follows them across devices, and changeable any time from Account → Settings → Streaming Service. Every service in that picker always works: it opens the exact release when GoodTunes has a stored deep link for it, and otherwise falls back to a per-service search for the artist + title (matching the artist-page "How to Play" sheet), so a fan is never blocked by a missing link. Operators paste each service's album/artist URL into its own labeled field in the admin editor. Masters we host still never leave as a file; a track we never hosted simply opens where it lives.
- **Sunset to streaming.** Every release can carry a "Sunset date" — the day it leaves its GoodTunes exclusive window and goes out to the streaming services. Before that date the album sells normally and its streaming links stay hidden. The moment the date arrives, the album page surfaces a "Listen on…" handoff to the fan's chosen service (Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, or Pandora) and the buy window closes — the Buy button reads "Sold Out", every format in the Buy sheet is disabled, and checkout is sealed end-to-end on the server so nothing can slip through. Fans who already own a copy keep their in-app access and can still play it. The operator sets the date and the six service URLs from a dedicated "Streaming" panel in the album editor (split out from the GoodTunes "Release" date), with the same one-click "Refresh links" auto-fill, and the admin Albums list moves the release into its **Sunset** tab automatically.
- **All six services on the artist-page "How to Play" sheet.** Tapping a release on an artist page opens an Apple-Music-style sheet that now lists all six streaming services — Apple Music, Spotify, Tidal, Qobuz, Deezer, and Pandora — instead of just Apple Music + Spotify. Each row uses the release's stored deep link when one exists and otherwise falls back to a per-service search for the artist + title, so a fan can always hand off to whichever service they use. Importing an album from Apple Music now also resolves the real per-release Spotify, Tidal, Deezer, and Pandora URLs automatically — Spotify via the Spotify Web API (matched by artist + title) and the other three via a free cross-service mapping — so those rows deep-link straight to the exact album instead of a search; Qobuz has no free per-release mapping and keeps the search fallback. For albums imported before that auto-resolution shipped (or where a lookup service was rate-limited at import time), the admin can refill the gaps without re-importing — a per-album "Refresh links" button on the album editor's Release panel, plus a one-click catalog-wide sweep in the Albums column that fills every release missing a link. The refresh resolves the same set the importer does — Spotify (Spotify Web API) alongside Tidal/Deezer/Pandora (cross-service mapping) — so a missing Spotify link gets backfilled too, not just the Odesli three. The catalog-wide sweep also backfills the artist-discography releases that power the "How to Play" sheet itself — so older artist pages start deep-linking to Tidal/Deezer/Pandora instead of searching, without re-pulling each artist's discography by hand. Because Spotify is matched by artist + title rather than the Apple album id, even a manually-created GoodTunes release that has no Apple Music URL at all can still get a real Spotify deep link from the same Refresh button and catalog sweep (Tidal/Deezer/Pandora stay on search for those releases, since the cross-service mapping needs the source Apple id).

## Buying, owning & gifting

Buy direct from the artist, get a numbered GoodDeed collectible, share or gift it, and track physical fulfillment — all leak-proof.

- **Dedicated launch storefront at `store.goodtunes.music`.** A fan who lands on the store host drops straight into the featured release's preview-and-purchase page — no menus, no login wall to look around — and the entire sign-in, preview, and embedded-checkout flow works there exactly as it does on the main fan host, so a release can be promoted on its own clean URL (used for the Nightbirde "Hope" launch). Deep links to any individual album keep working as before.

- **Clean per-release share links at `get.goodtunes.music/<name>`.** Every release can carry a short, human-readable share slug — `get.goodtunes.music/nightbirde` instead of a long UUID — that opens straight onto the same preview-and-buy page fans get from a normal album link, with no login wall. The operator sets the slug from a one-field panel on the album editor (live-normalized, uniqueness- and reserved-word-checked, with a one-tap "copy link" button and a one-tap "Suggest" button that proposes a clean, available slug derived from the artist/title), and the link rich-unfurls in iMessage/Slack/email with the album's real cover, title, and artist. Slugs only resolve for releases that are actually for sale — hidden, still-prepping, or removed releases return a normal not-found, so a leaked slug can never expose an unreleased album.

- **Legacy gogoods.com GoodDeed certificates regenerated.** Every imported gogoods.com owner gets a fresh, GoodTunes-branded GoodDeed certificate PDF — keyed off the original certificate number so #042 stays #042 — downloadable from the Library "Download GoodDeed" action on each imported copy. Paper size auto-picks Letter or A4 from the fan's shipping country, and the admin print queue tags these as "Legacy" so operators don't accidentally re-print certs fans already physically own.

- **Store-wide, leak-proof previews.** Fans (and visitors) can preview any song on any GoodTunes release before buying — even on hidden or unreleased albums, provided they have the link. Audio is delivered as the same encrypted adaptive-bitrate segments as the full master, but is hard-capped at the artist's chosen 30-second window on the server — so a preview session never exposes the full track file. Non-owners are never offered a "Stream on Spotify/Apple" handoff for a hosted GoodTunes track, keeping the focus on buying the high-res master.

- **Buy now, download later.** Fans can purchase an album from any device, and it's instantly added to their GoodTunes library across phone, tablet, and desktop. No file management or manual syncing.

- **Numbered GoodDeed® collectibles.** Every purchase mints a unique, numbered GoodDeed digital collectible. It's the fan's proof of ownership and a connection to the artist.

- **Physical fulfillment tracking.** For albums that include physical goods (vinyl, CDs, merch), fans can track the fulfillment status directly from their account.

- **Gifting.** Fans can buy an album as a gift for someone else. The recipient gets a unique link to claim their GoodDeed and add the album to their library.
